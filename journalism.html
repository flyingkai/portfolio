Journalism

While working for The Straits Times, I liked to say that I cover the “oddball beat”, because my writing is often difficult to categorise or define, while spanning a genuinely mind-numbing variety of beats.

Below is a selection of my longform journalism.

Examining everyday breakdowns and malfunction: Technology reporting is often fixated on innovation or high-profile collapse. My tech reporting delves into the far more mundane and oft-neglected practices that emerge around breakdowns and dysfunction.

  • ‘Not allowed to use the home Wi-Fi’: How domestic workers get online in Singapore
  • Inside OGP, the little-known tech unit behind RedeemSG, Parking.sg and ScamShield
  • Reddit wars: What’s driving Singapore’s largest online communities
  • Confessions of a S’pore Grab driver: How the app is changing as it enters its profitability era
  • Decades of image-based sexual abuse: How perpetrators evade tech platforms and the authorities
  • ‘Revenge porn’ victims speak out: ‘Will he take pictures of me when I’m not looking?’
  • When a mall grows old
  • Sky-high demolitions: What is behind Singapore’s many skyscraper tear-downs

Exploring the nature of narratives: A key interest of mine is examining the significance of the stories and histories we share, and imagining new ways to express them through experimental second-person journalism.

  • The story of you: What might Singapore look like for those born today?

  • This is how you're going to die?
  • Singapore without Singaporeans: What the island nation looks like on screen
  • ‘Do you kill children?’: Even before independence, S’pore has always loved its over-the-top campaigns

Understanding fringe subcultures: We live in the age of the infinite fringe, where subculture and counter-culture increasingly shape the mainstream. These explorations of fringe subcultures seek out hard-to-reach communities so they can explain in their own terms their worldview.

  • The crypto bros are back: ‘The hubris never really left’
  • Involuntarily celibate: Inside the minds of ‘incels’ who blame women and society
  • A furry cure for loneliness: ‘It’s like socialising on easy mode’
  • No sex, no problem? What it means to be asexual in Singapore
  • TikTok is paying S’pore Gen X ‘aunties’ to use app: ‘I’m at the stage where I thought I’d just try’
